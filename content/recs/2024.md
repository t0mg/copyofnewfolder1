# CopyOfNewFolder's recs archive 2024

## Genesis Noir

This weekend I tried Genesis Noir, a noir love story framed as the history of the universe. Mechanically the game is an almost wordless visual novel, where you explore small abstract environments, click on things to make something happen and sometimes engage with a basic game mechanic. It's broken into a bunch of chapters, each of which presents a different idea and a variation on the mode of interaction.

I found this game to be a mixed bag. Some chapters present you with pleasant toys to mess around with, some are just plain boring. One of them had minigames that were both too simple to be engaging, but too obscure to immediately figure out how to complete. A lot of the storytelling in the first three quarters of the game is mostly vague and wordless, done through analogy to cosmological events. The visual style is that of a cool graphic novel, but something is missing, and the game doesn't use it to its full potential for a lot of the time. I think that it comes together into something worthwhile in the final hour, but I didn't feel it for the rest of it.

If you like astrophysics or noir, consider picking it up with a deep discount.
https://store.steampowered.com/app/735290/Genesis_Noir/

## The Children of Clay

The Children of Clay is a short (~5 minutes) archaeological horror game about examining a clay statuette. If you are looking for something creepy, this is worth your time.
https://ronyaib.itch.io/the-children-of-clay

## Unavowed

This weekend I played Unavowed. It's a point and click adventure set in present day New York. You play as someone who just had a demon exorcised out of them, you don't remember what you did over the last year, and you join the eponymous organization, whose job is to protect the city from hostile supernatural happenings. So, you slowly remember what your old self did and try to correct the consequences of your past actions, as you try to resolve the different problems in the city.

The game's unique selling point is its branching. You can bring 2 companions into the different locations out of 4, each of which has different skills and abilities, which lead to different available interactions and solutions in the levels themselves. The game asks you to make a bunch of interesting decisions throughout its runtime, and I found the story engaging from the beginning. In gameplay there is more focus on dialog over puzzles.

In terms of negatives, in some parts you have to do fact finding, which involves moving between characters and exhausting their dialog trees, which unlocks conversation topics with other characters, and there's a bit too much of it. Voice recording quality is also very mixed: some voices are mixed in too quietly in the game, and there is one character in particular whose microphone quality is noticeably poor. The music is good though.

In short, if you like urban fantasy stories, do check this out.
https://store.steampowered.com/app/336140/Unavowed/

## Here's some stupid fun for this week: Destructivator 2 

Destructivator 2 is an retro side-scrolling shooter about a store-brand Terminator fighting off an alien invasion. It looks like something from MS-DOS or Amiga days, in that the story consists of a scrolling paragraph when you start the game and all you have to do is shoot everything that moves, but it also has much nicer color palettes and modern levels of polish. It's worth getting if you are looking for something retro and throwaway to unwind.
https://store.steampowered.com/app/1124990/Destructivator_2/

## Going Under

Going Under is an action roguelike in the style of Hades, where you are an unpaid intern working for a tech startup, delving into the basements of an office building to recover the forbidden tools of productivity from the failed companies based there. It's a dated satire of the Silicon Valley culture, because it uses the flat vector illustration art style that was everywhere at one point, and it came out before the GenAI boom. Gameplay wise it's ok: things work, but nothing stands out. Overall I found it mediocre, so I can't recommend it as anything other than as a time capsule of what the tech industry felt like at one point.
https://store.steampowered.com/app/1154810/Going_Under/

## Reliquia Park

Reliquia Park is a premium PuzzleScript game (in that it uses graphic tiles that are larger than 5x5 pixels) about solving block pushing puzzles. The unique twist here is that you have to figure out the rules for how to arrange the blocks yourself, and the game constantly challenges you with new rules and their combinations. There are also some narrative snippets about the different cultures that have faded into nothing in interesting ways, but otherwise it's a pure puzzle game with a password system to resume your progress. Also, the game is playable in the browser. Would recommend if you are looking for a thinky game.
https://parachor.itch.io/reliquiapark

## Doronko Wanko

Doronko Wanko is a free game about a dog, who systematically destroys their owners' property by spraying mud and paint everywhere in the full knowledge that it will be forgiven, because it is cute. It plays like a reverse Powerwash Simulator, and it's a fun distraction for half an hour. The game tries to pass this off as cute, but in my mind the dog causes enough damage to be put to sleep.
https://store.steampowered.com/app/2512840/DORONKO_WANKO/

## Catlateral Damage: Remeowstered

Catlateral Damage: Remeowstered is a game about a cat, who systematically destroys their owners' property by toppling anything and everything to the ground in the full knowledge that it will be forgiven, because it is cute. It's like you are the cat from A Little to the Left who has Had Enough. I wish people didn't infantilize their pets so much.
https://store.steampowered.com/app/1237730/Catlateral_Damage_Remeowstered
