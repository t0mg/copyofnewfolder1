# CopyOfNewFolder's recs archive 2026

## The Invincible
The Invincible is a video game adaptation of a Stanislaw Lem science fiction novel of the same name. The story concerns a group of scientists exploring a planet and discovering its peculiarities. I found that the science has moved on since the ideas introduced by the novel and its themes didn't grab me, but I liked the general story of scientists being professionals and the actual discovery of the planet's mystery.

Mechanically it's a walking simulator with a few diversions. It's well paced, most of the time you have something interesting to see or conversation to listen to, it's good at keeping your attention with its narrative. The art direction is the game's standout feature, it's set in 1960s retro futuristic scifi novel cover art with some really pretty rocky landscapes and bubbly machinery. I recommend this if you enjoy taking screenshots in games or are looking for a calmer, Star Trek-style story that lasts 6-7 hours.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/731040/The_Invincible/

## The Axis Unseen
This week I brought more landscape pornography to share. The Axis Unseen is a game where the Skyrim stealth archer goes on a hunting trip to a rock album cover. You skulk around hunting various beasts from different folklores with a bow, searching for new magical powers, equipment and scrolls from the previous visitors in a fantastical world built out of bones, jagged rocks and traversable 50 degree inclines. The bestiary starts with edgy deer and wolves and becomes increasingly esoteric and mechanically interesting. There isn't much actual hunting, but the changing opposition and the sound design (I recommend turning off the music) keep things fresh. I didn't connect with the writing, but I found the landscapes to be very pretty and well composed, exploring for screenshots kept me hooked the whole way through. It's important to note that the game includes the pacifist difficulty that changes the goal to take photos of the creatures in game, so you can play it like a spooky virtual hiking trip too.

I recommend this if you want something that plays like Skyrim, enjoy in-game photography, or if you would like to explore a metal interpretation of primal nature. It took me about 22 hours to finish it, but I was fairly exploration-happy.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1807810/The_Axis_Unseen/

## Babushka's Glitch Dungeon 
Babushka's Glitch Dungeon is a small puzzle platformer about exploring the titular dungeon that's experiencing NES-style graphics corruption. The game's main mechanic is switching between two different movement abilities to get around different obstacles, as well as using the local flora and fauna to your advantage. The game is not very demanding, it has some secrets to find and it has a unique visual style. It took me 3 hours to see and do everything, would recommend if you're looking for something quick and quirky.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1876850/Babushkas_Glitch_Dungeon/

## International Obsession
International Obsession is a free, short (~1 hour), surreal narrative game/3d platformer about a post-governmental humanity that has evolved the ability to double jump. It begins with you going to an exhibition/clearance sale of the ancient forgotten concept of Forbidding and goes wherever from there. It's by the author of Anthology of the Killer, and I like their particular silly presentation of soul searching and geopolitics. It soothed my anxieties somewhat, and the writing is fun to experience.

https://thecatamites.itch.io/international-obsession

## Dusklight
I've been playing Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess, and I wanted to highlight Dusklight, an unofficial reimplementation of the game on modern systems. You have to provide your own game disc image, and in return you get modern resolutions and controller support, uncapped framerates, quality of life features from the HD version and other goodies. Worth checking out if you're thinking of a replay.

https://twilitrealm.dev/

## Lorn's Lure
Lorn's Lure is a parkour game about exploring a concrete megastructure. It starts off with regular first-person platforming, but the game quickly provides you with climbing picks, then a wall kick, and your movement abilities keep escalating as the architecture becomes increasingly hostile for biped navigation. This game provides the exact opposite of yellow paint on climbable ledges, finding the path forward through incidental level geometry is half the challenge and, for me, a lot of the appeal. There is no combat. Platforming can get challenging, but there's a checkbox in the options menu that makes the levels more accessible. The game effectively conveys a sense of lonely exploration, the vibes are excellent. I would recommend this game if you want to explore an interesting space without being condescended to, or if you like the Blame! manga. It took me about 12 hours to finish the base game.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1417930/Lorns_Lure/

## Nainai's Recipe
Nainai's Recipe is a demo for a cooking game set in China, just before the Covid lockdowns (and during them in the full game, presumably). This is the most detailed simulation of cooking that I came across. You can manually dice the ingredients with a cleaver and they split at the exact line of the cut, you can turn them in a wok using direct mouse movements, you can add oil and soy sauce by eye, the ingredients cook in individual pieces and can only tell when they're done through visual inspection. And then you take a picture of your creation and have your grandmother judge you. It's not clear if the project was abandoned or not, but it is a very interesting attempt to make a cooking system that you can transfer your real world cooking skills to.

https://houkanshan.itch.io/nainais-recipe

## Everhood 2
Everhood 2 is a rhythm game adjacent RPG about exploring strange places and meeting a wide variety of characters. During combat you move your avatar over five tracks, dodging incoming notes, absorbing notes of the same color to charge your attacks, and trying to see through the many different ways your opponents mess with the screen to get you to make a mistake. The soundtrack is almost 6 hours long on Youtube and the game has the number of different encounters to match.

For me the game's weak point is the deliberate(?) absence of story. You get a goal to work towards at the beginning, but it falls away, and then you just meander around sightseeing and following along with whatever is happening. The first game had a much stronger hook, and though you can interpret the events of the sequel to match the themes of the series, you still have to experience events happening at you with no end goal in sight. The actual places and characters in the game are fun to see and talk to, just don't expect the plot to carry the game if you don't like the mechanics.

I finished it in about 12 hours, would recommend it if you rhythm games, or are looking for something quirky and unusual. The original game is very much worth experiencing too, especially if you're looking for something Undertale-adjacent.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1984020/Everhood_2/

## BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER 3: I OPENED A PORTAL TO HELL IN THE FAVELA TRYING TO REVIVE MIT AIA I NEED TO CLOSE IT 
BRAZILIAN DRUG DEALER 3: I OPENED A PORTAL TO HELL IN THE FAVELA TRYING TO REVIVE MIT AIA I NEED TO CLOSE IT is an unapologetically Brazilian FPS that celebrates their culture and is full incomprehensible in-jokes and memes. ## Cartomancy Anthology
Cartomancy Anthology is a free collection of games from different developers, each of which has been inspired by a different tarot card. Each one lasts about 10-30 minutes, I sampled about half of them and found some worthwhile experiments. It's well worth trying if you're looking for a palate cleanser or are short on time.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1869870/Cartomancy_Anthology/

## Parking Garage Rally Circuit 
Parking Garage Rally Circuit is a very Sega-inspired racing game about getting the best lap time in various parking garages. The controls are simple but fun to master, you only have acceleration/brakes and Mario Kart-style drifts that give you speed. The game itself consists of 8 tracks (+8 more with DLC) and 3 vehicle classes, colorful visuals and an upbeat ska soundtrack. It's pure, dumb fun, but there isn't much game outside of chasing leaderboard positions, and it took me about 3.5 hours to get gold medals in all events of the base game. Would recommend this as a brief, uplifting palate cleanser.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/2737300/Parking_Garage_Rally_Circuit/
