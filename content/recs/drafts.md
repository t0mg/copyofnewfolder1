## The Invincible
The Invincible is a video game adaptation of a Stanislaw Lem science fiction novel of the same name. The story concerns a group of scientists exploring a planet and discovering its peculiarities. I found that the science has moved on since the ideas introduced by the novel and its themes didn't grab me, but I liked the general story of scientists being professionals and the actual discovery of the planet's mystery.

Mechanically it's a walking simulator with a few diversions. It's well paced, most of the time you have something interesting to see or conversation to listen to, it's good at keeping your attention with its narrative. The art direction is the game's standout feature, it's set in 1960s retro futuristic scifi novel cover art with some really pretty rocky landscapes and bubbly machinery. I recommend this if you enjoy taking screenshots in games or are looking for a calmer, Star Trek-style story that lasts 6-7 hours.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/731040/The_Invincible/

## The Axis Unseen
This week I brought more landscape pornography to share. The Axis Unseen is a game where the Skyrim stealth archer goes on a hunting trip to a rock album cover. You skulk around hunting various beasts from different folklores with a bow, searching for new magical powers, equipment and scrolls from the previous visitors in a fantastical world built out of bones, jagged rocks and traversable 50 degree inclines. The bestiary starts with edgy deer and wolves and becomes increasingly esoteric and mechanically interesting. There isn't much actual hunting, but the changing opposition and the sound design (I recommend turning off the music) keep things fresh. I didn't connect with the writing, but I found the landscapes to be very pretty and well composed, exploring for screenshots kept me hooked the whole way through. It's important to note that the game includes the pacifist difficulty that changes the goal to take photos of the creatures in game, so you can play it like a spooky virtual hiking trip too.

I recommend this if you want something that plays like Skyrim, enjoy in-game photography, or if you would like to explore a metal interpretation of primal nature. It took me about 22 hours to finish it, but I was fairly exploration-happy.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1807810/The_Axis_Unseen/

## Babushka's Glitch Dungeon 
Babushka's Glitch Dungeon is a small puzzle platformer about exploring the titular dungeon that's experiencing NES-style graphics corruption. The game's main mechanic is switching between two different movement abilities to get around different obstacles, as well as using the local flora and fauna to your advantage. The game is not very demanding, it has some secrets to find and it has a unique visual style. It took me 3 hours to see and do everything, would recommend if you're looking for something quick and quirky.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1876850/Babushkas_Glitch_Dungeon/


International Obsession is a free, short (~1 hour), surreal narrative game/3d platformer about a post-governmental humanity that has evolved the ability to double jump. It begins with you going to an exhibition/clearance sale of the ancient forgotten concept of Forbidding and goes wherever from there. It's by the author of Anthology of the Killer, and I like their particular silly presentation of soul searching and geopolitics. It soothed my anxieties somewhat, and the writing is fun to experience.

https://thecatamites.itch.io/international-obsession

## Dusklight
I've been playing Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess, and I wanted to highlight Dusklight, an unofficial reimplementation of the game on modern systems. You have to provide your own game disc image, and in return you get modern resolutions and controller support, uncapped framerates, quality of life features from the HD version and other goodies. Worth checking out if you're thinking of a replay.

https://twilitrealm.dev/
