## Orbo's Odyssey {time: 2026-06-28T16:00:00.000Z}
Orbo's Odyssey is a short 3d platformer about a little scrunkly man who accidentally locked himself inside his boss' office. He is now on a quest to collect some gears to 3d print himself an arm he can then use to rotate the office door knob and leave. In gameplay Orbo can fire himself forward like a bullet and do some bunny hopping to maintain speed. The moveset and platforming don't really have anywhere to evolve since the bullet boost is that overpowered, but the game is short enough that it doesn't become a problem. I found the game's areas and writing fun to explore, with endearing text and some sticky details inside the levels (e.g. sniper rifle equipped Draculas keeping you inside the mission area). The music consists entirely of 2000s Drum and Bass. You can rinse this game in about 1 hour, would recommend it if you want to dip into something with great feeling movement for a bit.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/2539960/Orbos_Odyssey/

## Later Alligator {time: 2026-06-29T16:00:00.000Z}
In Later Alligator you help an anxiety-ridden alligator Pat calm his nerves by investigating his family, who will throw him a birthday party/murder him for betraying the mafia this evening. Mechanically it's a collection of minigames, connected with a point and click adventure.

The minigames themselves are a mixed bag, they're sometimes funny, mostly mediocre, and there's amazingly terrible pinball that demonstrates everything I dislike about pinball. However, they are also used to develop the characters they are attached to. You get to meet Pat's entire extended family in the Alligator New-York and help them with their individual woes, and they are written and animated in an endearing and often funny manner. The writing carries this game, and it helps the few frustrations I had with it to fade into the background (unless you want to get all the achievements). I also found the game's music loops a bit grating, but that's neither here nor there.

I finished the game, got all the collectibles and the true ending in about 4.5 hours. I would recommend this if you're looking for something warm, relaxed and family-friendly.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/966320/Later_Alligator/

## Tactical Breach Wizards {time: 2026-06-30T16:00:00.000Z}
Do you know the door breach sequences in Call of Duty? Where you place a breaching charge on a door, it explodes, and then you shoot every bad guy in a room while the time is slowed down? Tactical Breach Wizards is that sequence turned into a full tactics puzzle, but with militarized wizards instead of elite soldiers.

Mechanically, this is Into the Breach if it was a puzzle game. Each of your units can do one movement and one action per turn, and you can see what every enemy unit is going to do at the end of the current turn. Unlike the other titles in the genre, you also have unlimited undo up to the beginning of the turn that lets you experiment. The abilities of each of your wizards are likewise designed to interact in interesting ways, and so the game wants you to solve each position and orchestrate crazy plays to get perfect turns, instead of forcing you to commit to your actions. This is fun.

The plot also has lots to like. It plays out like a typical geopolitical thriller, but in which humanity has successfully militarized magic and now it's just another kind of technology used by people and governments for good and ill. It's well thought out, and I wouldn't mind another game in this setting. I found the prose to be funny and well-made, with good Deadpool-style banter that is delivered by actually likable middle-aged messes of characters, instead of some obnoxious main character types.

It took me about 30 hours (so, shorter than the length of an XCOM campaign) to 100% the game on the harder difficulty preset. Tactical Breach Wizards is friendly with its difficulty: you can freely tune how forgiving the game is, and you can skip any cutscene or level that you may find frustrating without the game judging you. I would recommend this to people who would like to get into tactics games, genre veterans who like the tactical puzzle itself, and people who wished Into the Breach wasn't a roguelike in particular.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1043810/Tactical_Breach_Wizards/

## I Am Your Beast {time: 2026-07-01T16:00:00.000Z}
I Am Your Beast is about a local John Wick type who resigns his job in the US Military. John's commanding officer doesn't take this decision well, so John must spend the entire game fertilizing the forest with the soldiers sent after him.

This is an FPS about speedrunning through small (~60 second) levels to get the lowest completion time, similar to something like Neon White. Killing enemies reduces the time on the clock, so it's not just about beelining towards the level's objectives. The story campaign consists of more open areas that you'll have to plot an optimal path through, and the challenge levels tend more towards prescriptive linear gauntlets. You'll get the most out of this game if you enjoy chasing ranks in challenge modes with all the repetition it implies, as without it this is just a short shooter with a standout soundtrack.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1876590/I_Am_Your_Beast/

## Prodigal
Prodigal is a 2d Zelda-like about a thief returning to his community and making amends. Prodigal is heavily inspired by the Gameboy Legend of Zelda games: you explore dungeons to find items, defeat bosses that are defeated with the said item, and then you go find another dungeon while helping the townsfolk in between.

The first half of the game is a pretty formulaic Zelda adventure (that I found on the easier side) where the protagonist goes through his narrative arc and saves the day. You can end the game right there with a satisfying ending, but what's unusual is that there's another half of the game after the credits. This is a separate story arc that is centered more on the affairs of the settlement, how you become a part of its community, and a dive into the world's lore. There are more areas to explore and items to find, it's not just more side quests. I think I only ever saw Nier structure itself like this. And there are more things to find after that's done too, if you like digging for secrets.

Overall I found it enjoyable, and it took me about 7 hours for each of the two main endings. It could be more polished, but this was the team's first published project and it's a good effort. Would recommend it if a more forgiving Mina the Hollower sounds fun to you.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1393820/Prodigal/
