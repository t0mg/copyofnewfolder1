## Go Mecha Ball {time: 2026-06-11T16:00:00.000Z}

Go Mecha Ball is twin-stick shooter roguelite, where you have Sonic's spindash as one of your core movement abilities. The dash can be used both defensively to evade attacks and reposition, and offensively to interrupt enemy attacks and regain ammo. The movement is very polished and it's fun to be an angry pinball when you figure it out, but there's little to find outside of the main gameplay loop systems-wise. The art style is colorful and fun, and the sounds and music convey the mood excellently, it's easy to lose yourself in the moment if you want to. If you are looking for an actiony run-based game that takes up 40 minutes per attempt, try Go Mecha Ball.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/2008510/Go_Mecha_Ball/

## FAR {time: 2026-06-12T16:00:00.000Z}

I played through the FAR duology of games last week. FAR: Lone Sails is basically Limbo, but with a car: you are a small child in a big empty grey world, and you have to operate a steam powered locomotive to the best of your ability to get to a vague destination somewhere to the right. Meanwhile FAR: Changing Tides is Inside but with a boat: you are a small child in a big empty desaturated world and you have to operate a steam powered ship to the best of your ability to get to a vague destination somewhere on the right. In both games you collect fuel for your vehicles and maintain them, stop to explore the structures along the way and gawp at the scenery passing by. Like their inspirations, there isn't much of a plot in either of the games (though the second ones have background lore), they are instead melancholy mood pieces involving searching for something in a big uncaring world. I recommend both of them if you are looking for something slower and atmospheric you can lose yourself in, and both games are very screenshotable.

https://store.steampowered.com/bundle/24953/FAR_Universe_Bundle/

## Harmless Drudge {time: 2026-06-13T16:00:00.000Z}

As for video games, I tried Harmless Drudge this weekend. It's a game that asks you to provide definitions for words in a dictionary, but it's actually about defining operations in a stack-based virtual machine in terms of other operations. Unlike other programming games, you are not building up complexity bottom-up, instead you are using the already existing full functionality of the programming language to define its operators. What it means in practice is circular definitions everywhere. If you are looking for a different take on a programming challenge, try this one. Just have some pen and paper ready, this gets involved fast.

https://qwtyest.itch.io/harmless-drudge

## Wavetale {time: 2026-06-15T16:00:00.000Z}

Wavetale is a 3d platformer with a strong focus on narrative, where you surf the waves trying to mitigate the effects of a recent ecological disaster. The story is well-acted, it has a satisfying narrative arc, and it drives the game forward as there are few things to distract you from going to the next waypoint. I liked this, because methodically scouring levels for collectibles can sometimes feel like work. The controls are tight, the game provides a good sense of speed and scale, and overall it makes the protagonist feel good to control. The combat is underdeveloped, but the encounters are brief and easy enough that it never has enough time to grate. I enjoyed the 5 hours I spent getting through this game, would recommend this if you are looking for a quick narrative experience with some actual game mechanics.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1823930/Wavetale/

## Yellow Taxi Goes Vroom {time: 2026-06-16T16:00:00.000Z}

Yellow Taxi Goes Vroom is a collectathon platformer, where you control a wind-up taxi that can spindash and flop on its roof. It's Crazy Taxi crossed with Super Mario Odyssey, a bright and cheerful game about constantly launching yourself off of sloped pieces of geometry in order to collect green gears, with the end goal of stopping Alien Mosk, the evil CEO of Tosla. It's an easy to learn, hard to master kind of game, as its controls really are just steering and spindash, with difficulty coming from accurately landing your jumps on increasingly precarious ledges. I found the humor of the story to be hit and miss, though it's pretty funny that every single lawyer NPC drops the same legal disclaimer that any similarities to real things are strictly accidental. It took me ~12 hours to 100% it, though you can probably get through to the credits in about 8. I had fun, would recommend if you are looking for something vaguely Mario-shaped to unwind to.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/2011780/Yellow_Taxi_Goes_Vroom/

## The Immortal John Triptych {time: 2026-06-18T16:00:00.000Z}

The Immortal John Triptych is a trilogy of point and click adventure games set inside renaissance paintings. Their plots and writing are Monty Python style absurdism that mixes the high cultural weight of its inspirations with low-brow humor (and disrespect of the christian Church in particular), and overall I found them to be pretty funny. For example, in the first game you are speedrunning the seven deadly sins for Church bureaucracy reasons. The puzzles are mostly reasonable, though I found the inventory a bit unwieldy. I found the third game to be weaker, but it also has the most cathartic ending. Each game also has a gallery that contains all of the paintings that were used to build it, so they are arguably educational. In short, I would recommend these to GAC as a whole, and you can finish each game in about 3 hours.

https://store.steampowered.com/curator/41213029-The-Immortal-John-Triptych/

## Curse Crackers: For Whom The Belle Toils {time: 2026-06-19T16:00:00.000Z}

Curse Crackers: For Whom the Belle Toils is a Game Boy Color inspired 2d platformer and yet another hidden gem that needs wider recognition. As far as basics go, the controls are satisfying and responsive, moving (especially fast) feels good and I always felt that any failures were my fault and not the game's. The game's unique mechanical feature is your bell-shaped companion that acts as an attack projectile and a bouncing platform, and he's fun to use. The difficulty starts off light and it steadily grows, becoming not too demanding by the end of the main story. The game keeps changing the level gimmicks and environments, so it never grows stale either. It has straightforward GB-era sensibilities, and I would recommend it as a straight platformer if you like the genre.

On top of this, the game distinguishes itself in two ways. First, the game is built around secrets. There are hidden collectibles in the levels, and extra hidden puzzles and quests if you really want to look for them. It's no Void Stranger, but it can satisfy the same itch, and I'm still working on the post-game content and mysteries. Second, this game has very girly sensibilities. The main story is about your rival stealing your boyfriend, all the quests and errands you do are about improving social bonds or trying to be helpful, other characters (majority of whom are women) have similar concerns, dating gets mentioned by them at all, and the post-game story seems to point towards the toxicity of being overly positive. It's not twee or cynically pink either, and it's worth flagging given how underserved the niche is.

Would recommend this game if you are looking for a good platformer, especially if you are a completionist looking for something satisfying to dig into. Howlongtobeat says that it's ~5 hours long if you mainline it, and I'm currently 17 hours in with an 87% save file. It's good.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1726760/Curse_Crackers_For_Whom_the_Belle_Toils/

## Terminal Lucidity {time: 2026-06-20T16:00:00.000Z}

Terminal Lucidity is a game about reverse engineering string ciphers. To progress, you have to send passwords to door locks. The locks are connected to computer terminals, each of which mangles the incoming text in some way before passing it on. The challenge is to figure out exactly what each computer does, so that you can make sure that the lock receives the correct plain text password. The computer terminals can be chained in a row, and later you learn how to change how the machines are connected in order to unlock more demanding locks. Everything in the game is gated by knowledge, you don't unlock any abilities that let you do things you couldn't at the start.

However, this is only half of the game. The other half is looking for clues in a 3d environment, interrogating the terminals themselves and trying to figure out the sometimes cryptic hints the locks provide. I had to look up the guide because I missed some post-it notes in the concrete basement you find yourself in, but I had fun figuring out the black boxes that each terminal presents. There is also a greater mystery, but I missed even more post-its and I wasn't in the mood to reopen everything again. I would recommend this game to anyone engineering-minded, just be aware that writing down notes and solutions is mandatory as the game doesn't save in-game progress. Also, the game is free, and it took me ~6 hours to get to the first ending.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/4154810/Terminal_Lucidity/

## Lunistice {time: 2026-06-21T16:00:00.000Z}

## Solas 128 {time: 2026-06-22T16:00:00.000Z}

Solas 128 is a puzzle game about redirecting lasers. It does two things that set it apart from similar kinds of games: first, you are directing the movements of individual photons, which introduces timing into the solution space. Secondly, the game presents a large interconnected puzzle, and later on solutions involve thinking about multiple puzzle rooms at the same time. It's a good brain teaser, would recommend if you're looking for something like it right now.

## Trepang2 {time: 2026-06-25T16:00:00.000Z}

Trepang2 is an FPS about an illuminati super soldier clone who dismantles Amazon one anomalous object at a time. It's a straightforward shooter directly inspired by F.E.A.R. (i.e. it has lots of slow motion Matrixy combat in corporate and industrial environments against spec ops bad guys with some supernatural horror elements), but with modern additions (schmovement, invisibility cloak, some ideas from Call of Duties, SCP-inspired horror instead of The Ring). This game's main combat loop is fun while the novelty lasts, but I found that it gets a bit one note. But since Trepang2 was made by a team of 4 and with surprisingly high production values, that's understandable. You'll have fun painting walls with raspberry jam, finish the game in about ~6 hours for all the campaign and side missions, and then put it away satisfied. If you're looking for an FPS built around a short single player campaign, this one is worth experiencing.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1164940/Trepang2/

## Northern Journey {time: 2026-06-28T16:00:00.000Z}

Northern Journey is a game about hiking in mythological Scandinavia, as you carry out an ordeal for the crime of passing by the wrong fjord in a row boat. Mechanically, this is an FPS with early 2000s-level graphics and metroidvania-like level progression. In practice, this is one of the most atmospheric games I've played recently. 

The environments immediately evoke a walk through a damp forest after it just rained, and every single area in the game is photogenic and incredibly screenshottable. Furthermore, the game runs on the old folklore logic: the nature is uncaring and every level feels carved into a landscape rather than being designed for optimal gameplay experience, and the story resembles a dark tale from an isolated community where you have to deal with malicious spirits, objects of superstition and inexplicable magic. It hits the pre-modern mindset and there's a lot to be immersed in, you can clearly tell that the author likes hiking.

There is also combat. For the most part it's there to provide friction to make exploration more interesting, but unfortunately it also has some boss fights that tend to degenerate into S+M1 in confined spaces. This can get frustrating, so make sure to adjust the difficulty slider in the options menu if it's a problem. You should also avoid this game if you hate insects and especially spiders, since man-sized crawlies are most of your opposition.

Overall, I liked this game a lot and I would recommend it to anyone who would like to be immersed in untamed wilderness for a while. It took me about 12 hours to finish it.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1639790/Northern_Journey/
