## Go Mecha Ball {time: 2026-06-11T16:00:00.000Z}
Go Mecha Ball is twin-stick shooter roguelite, where you have Sonic's spindash as one of your core movement abilities.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/2008510/Go_Mecha_Ball/

---
The dash can be used both defensively to evade attacks and reposition, and offensively to interrupt enemy attacks and regain ammo. The movement is very polished and it's fun to be an angry pinball when you figure it out, but there's little to find outside of the main gameplay loop systems-wise. The art style is colorful and fun, and the sounds and music convey the mood excellently, it's easy to lose yourself in the moment if you want to. If you are looking for an actiony run-based game that takes up 40 minutes per attempt, try Go Mecha Ball.

## Harmless Drudge {time: 2026-06-13T16:00:00.000Z}
I tried Harmless Drudge. It's a game that asks you to provide definitions for words in a dictionary, but it's actually about defining operations in a stack-based virtual machine in terms of other operations.

https://qwtyest.itch.io/harmless-drudge

---
Unlike other programming games, you are not building up complexity bottom-up, instead you are using the already existing full functionality of the programming language to define its operators. What it means in practice is circular definitions everywhere. If you are looking for a different take on a programming challenge, try this one. Just have some pen and paper ready, this gets involved fast.

## Wavetale {time: 2026-06-15T16:00:00.000Z}
Wavetale is a 3d platformer with a strong focus on narrative, where you surf the waves trying to mitigate the effects of a recent ecological disaster.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1823930/Wavetale/

---
The story is well-acted, it has a satisfying narrative arc, and it drives the game forward as there are few things to distract you from going to the next waypoint. I liked this, because methodically scouring levels for collectibles can sometimes feel like work. The controls are tight, the game provides a good sense of speed and scale, and overall it makes the protagonist feel good to control. ## Curse Crackers: For Whom The Belle Toils {time: 2026-06-19T16:00:00.000Z}
Curse Crackers: For Whom the Belle Toils is a Game Boy Color inspired 2d platformer and yet another hidden gem that needs wider recognition.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1726760/Curse_Crackers_For_Whom_the_Belle_Toils/

---
As far as basics go, the controls are satisfying and responsive, moving (especially fast) feels good and I always felt that any failures were my fault and not the game's. The game's unique mechanical feature is your bell-shaped companion that acts as an attack projectile and a bouncing platform, and he's fun to use. The difficulty starts off light and it steadily grows, becoming not too demanding by the end of the main story. The game keeps changing the level gimmicks and environments, so it never grows stale either. It has straightforward GB-era sensibilities, and I would recommend it as a straight platformer if you like the genre.

![A Game Boy Color style pixel-art screenshot from Curse Crackers showing the protagonist, Belle, with her purple companion Chime, talking to Fione in a grassy camping area, with a dialogue box at the bottom where Belle says: "YES FIONE. I'VE TOUCHED GRASS. MANY TIMES."](screenshots/curse-crackers.png)

On top of this, the game distinguishes itself in two ways. First, the game is built around secrets. There are hidden collectibles in the levels, and extra hidden puzzles and quests if you really want to look for them. It's no Void Stranger, but it can satisfy the same itch, and I'm still working on the post-game content and mysteries. Second, this game has very girly sensibilities. The main story is about your rival stealing your boyfriend, all the quests and errands you do are about improving social bonds or trying to be helpful, other characters (majority of whom are women) have similar concerns, dating gets mentioned by them at all, and the post-game story seems to point towards the toxicity of being overly positive. It's not twee or cynically pink either, and it's worth flagging given how underserved the niche is.

Would recommend this game if you are looking for a good platformer, especially if you are a completionist looking for something satisfying to dig into. Howlongtobeat says that it's ~5 hours long if you mainline it, and I'm currently 17 hours in with an 87% save file. It's good.

## Terminal Lucidity {time: 2026-06-20T16:00:00.000Z}
Terminal Lucidity is a game about reverse engineering string ciphers.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/4154810/Terminal_Lucidity/

---
To progress, you have to send passwords to door locks. The locks are connected to computer terminals, each of which mangles the incoming text in some way before passing it on. The challenge is to figure out exactly what each computer does, so that you can make sure that the lock receives the correct plain text password. The computer terminals can be chained in a row, and later you learn how to change how the machines are connected in order to unlock more demanding locks. Everything in the game is gated by knowledge, you don't unlock any abilities that let you do things you couldn't at the start.

![A green-screen CRT terminal in Terminal Lucidity showing a first-person perspective of logged text messages about a character named Angel trying to maintain a light in a dark, godless world.](screenshots/terminal-lucidity.png)

However, this is only half of the game. The other half is looking for clues in a 3d environment, interrogating the terminals themselves and trying to figure out the sometimes cryptic hints the locks provide. I had to look up the guide because I missed some post-it notes in the concrete basement you find yourself in, but I had fun figuring out the black boxes that each terminal presents. There is also a greater mystery, but I missed even more post-its and I wasn't in the mood to reopen everything again.

I would recommend this game to anyone engineering-minded, just be aware that writing down notes and solutions is mandatory as the game doesn't save in-game progress. Also, the game is free, and it took me ~6 hours to get to the first ending.

## Northern Journey {time: 2026-06-27T16:00:00.000Z}
Northern Journey is a game about hiking in mythological Scandinavia, as you carry out an ordeal for the crime of passing by the wrong fjord in a row boat. 

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1639790/Northern_Journey/

---
Mechanically, this is an FPS with early 2000s-level graphics and metroidvania-like level progression. In practice, this is one of the most atmospheric games I've played recently. 
![A first-person view looking down a narrow rope suspension bridge over a rushing river cascading down waterfalls in a steep, forested valley.](screenshots/northern-journey.png)

The environments immediately evoke a walk through a damp forest after it just rained, and every single area in the game is photogenic and incredibly screenshottable. Furthermore, the game runs on the old folklore logic: the nature is uncaring and every level feels carved into a landscape rather than being designed for optimal gameplay experience, and the story resembles a dark tale from an isolated community where you have to deal with malicious spirits, objects of superstition and inexplicable magic. It hits the pre-modern mindset and there's a lot to be immersed in, you can clearly tell that the author likes hiking.
![A dark volcanic canyon path at night with a glowing orange river of lava flowing under gnarled, burnt, leafless trees and a starry night sky.](screenshots/northern-journey-2.png)
![A long stone arch viaduct crossing a snowy mountain pass during a heavy blizzard under a thick grey fog.](screenshots/northern-journey-3.png)

There is also combat. For the most part it's there to provide friction to make exploration more interesting, but unfortunately it also has some boss fights that tend to degenerate into S+M1 in confined spaces. This can get frustrating, so make sure to adjust the difficulty slider in the options menu if it's a problem. You should also avoid this game if you hate insects and especially spiders, since man-sized crawlies are most of your opposition.
![Inside a dark, moss-covered cave filled with hanging stalactites and rising stalagmites, looking out towards a glowing blue abyss.](screenshots/northern-journey-4.png)

Overall, I liked this game a lot and I would recommend it to anyone who would like to be immersed in untamed wilderness for a while. It took me about 12 hours to finish it.

## Tactical Breach Wizards {time: 2026-06-30T16:00:00.000Z}
Do you know the door breach sequences in Call of Duty? Where you place a breaching charge on a door, it explodes, and then you shoot every bad guy in a room while the time is slowed down?

![A screenshot from Tactical Breach Wizards showing two armed wizards chatting as they are about to breach a door. "what does it pay?" "Nothing. I'm broke." "That's actually very competitive with what I'm earning right now". ](screenshots/tactical-breach-wizards.png)

---

Tactical Breach Wizards is that sequence turned into a full tactics puzzle, but with militarized wizards instead of elite soldiers.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1043810/Tactical_Breach_Wizards/

---
Mechanically, this is Into the Breach if it was a puzzle game. Each of your units can do one movement and one action per turn, and you can see what every enemy unit is going to do at the end of the current turn. Unlike the other titles in the genre, you also have unlimited undo up to the beginning of the turn that lets you experiment. The abilities of each of your wizards are likewise designed to interact in interesting ways, and so the game wants you to solve each position and orchestrate crazy plays to get perfect turns, instead of forcing you to commit to your actions. This is fun.

---
The plot also has lots to like. It plays out like a typical geopolitical thriller, but in which humanity has successfully militarized magic and now it's just another kind of technology used by people and governments for good and ill. It's well thought out, and I wouldn't mind another game in this setting. I found the prose to be funny and well-made, with good Deadpool-style banter that is delivered by actually likable middle-aged messes of characters, instead of some obnoxious main character types.

---
It took me about 30 hours (so, shorter than the length of an XCOM campaign) to 100% the game on the harder difficulty preset. Tactical Breach Wizards is friendly with its difficulty: you can freely tune how forgiving the game is, and you can skip any cutscene or level that you may find frustrating without the game judging you. I would recommend this to people who would like to get into tactics games, genre veterans who like the tactical puzzle itself, and people who wished Into the Breach wasn't a roguelike in particular.

## I Am Your Beast {time: 2026-07-01T16:00:00.000Z}

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1876590/I_Am_Your_Beast/

## Prodigal {time: 2026-07-02T16:00:00.000Z}
Prodigal is a 2d Zelda-like about a thief returning to his community and making amends.

https://store.steampowered.com/app/1393820/Prodigal/

---
Prodigal is heavily inspired by the Gameboy Legend of Zelda games: you explore dungeons to find items, defeat bosses that are defeated with the said item, and then you go find another dungeon while helping the townsfolk in between.

---
The first half of the game is a pretty formulaic Zelda adventure (that I found on the easier side) where the protagonist goes through his narrative arc and saves the day. You can end the game right there with a satisfying ending, but what's unusual is that there's another half of the game after the credits. This is a separate story arc that is centered more on the affairs of the settlement, how you become a part of its community, and a dive into the world's lore. There are more areas to explore and items to find, it's not just more side quests. I think I only ever saw Nier structure itself like this. And there are more things to find after that's done too, if you like digging for secrets.

---
Overall I found it enjoyable, and it took me about 7 hours for each of the two main endings. It could be more polished, but this was the team's first published project and it's a good effort. Would recommend it if a more forgiving Mina the Hollower sounds fun to you.
